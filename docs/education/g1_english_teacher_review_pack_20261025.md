# Unit 1 review: Greetings — Say Hello and Goodbye

# Greetings: Say Hello and Goodbye
**Grade 1 English | Original MELA draft | NOT textbook-verified | Teacher review required**

## We will learn
- Say **hello** and **goodbye** politely.
- Tell someone our name and ask for their name.
- Ask **How are you?** and give a simple answer.
- Choose a greeting for morning or afternoon.

## Words and expressions
hello · goodbye · good morning · good afternoon · my name is · what is your name? · how are you? · I am fine · thank you

## Warm-up (3 minutes)
The teacher smiles and waves: "**Hello!**" Children reply: "**Hello!**" Wave again and say "**Goodbye!**" Invite pairs to repeat. A child may point, wave, sign, or speak.

## Listen and say (7 minutes)
The teacher reads slowly; children repeat one line at a time.

**Hana:** Hello! My name is Hana. What is your name?

**Sami:** Hello! My name is Sami.

**Hana:** How are you?

**Sami:** I am fine, thank you. How are you?

**Hana:** I am fine, thank you.

**Sami:** Goodbye, Hana!

**Hana:** Goodbye, Sami!

Point out: **What is your name?** asks a question. **My name is ...** gives an answer. We greet people kindly, but nobody must share personal details beyond a first name in an exercise.

## Try together (5 minutes)
The teacher says a time and children choose a greeting:
- When we meet someone in the morning: "**Good morning!**"
- When we meet someone in the afternoon: "**Good afternoon!**"
- When we leave: "**Goodbye!**"
- Before going to sleep, we might say "**Good night!**" (not a greeting for meeting someone).

## Your turn (6 minutes)
Work with a partner or a puppet. Say hello. Ask the partner's name. Ask how they are. Say goodbye. Children who prefer not to speak may point to greeting cards. Swap roles.

Write or trace:
1. Hello!
2. My name is ______.
3. Goodbye!

## Check your learning (4 minutes)
1. What do you say when you meet your friend? **Hello.**
2. What do you ask to learn a friend's name? **What is your name?**
3. What do you say when you leave? **Goodbye.**
4. Put in order: Goodbye / Hello / What is your name? **Hello → What is your name? → Goodbye.**

## Teacher notes
Model short sentences, repeat them, and accept age-appropriate pronunciation attempts. Use role-play instead of asking children to memorise written dialogue. Be sensitive to learners with hearing or speech differences. Invite a supportive peer to model; allow gestures and picture cards.

# Unit 2 review: Parts of the Body — Name and Describe

# Parts of the Body
**Grade 1 English | Original MELA draft | NOT textbook-verified | Teacher review required**

## We will learn
- Say common English body-part words.
- Match a body-part word with a familiar use.
- Use **one** and **two** with everyday examples.

## Words
head · eyes · ears · nose · mouth · hands · arms · legs · feet · see · hear · smell · walk · hold

## Warm-up (3 minutes)
Show a simple drawing of a person. Ask children what they can name in any language. Say the English word and invite a reply. Children may point to a picture rather than touch themselves.

## Listen and say (7 minutes)
The teacher points to the drawing and reads:
- "**These are my eyes.**" We use our eyes to see.
- "**These are my ears.**" We use our ears to hear.
- "**This is my nose.**" We use our nose to smell.
- "**This is my mouth.**" We use our mouth to eat and speak.
- "**These are my hands.**" We use our hands to hold things.
- "**These are my feet.**" Feet can help us walk.

Talk about language: **This is** points to one thing. **These are** points to more than one.

## Try together (6 minutes)
Match a word card to a drawing: **eyes, ears, nose, mouth, hands, feet**. Then match the actions: **see, hear, smell, eat, hold, walk**. Each child can choose to speak, point or use picture symbols.

Practice sentences:
- I have **two hands**.
- I have **two ears**.
- This is **my nose**.
- These are **my feet**.

Bodies are different: people use their bodies in different ways, and everyone belongs in the activity. Do not correct a child's personal experience; we are practising common English words.

## Your turn (5 minutes)
1. Draw or look at a person and label **eyes**, **nose** and **hands**.
2. Finish: "I see with my ______." (**eyes**)
3. Finish: "I hear with my ______." (**ears**)
4. Circle the plural: **hand / hands** (**hands**).

## Check your learning (4 minutes)
Teacher says a word, child shows the matching picture:
- **nose** → nose
- **feet** → feet
- **mouth** → mouth
The child completes: "**These are my hands.**"

## Teacher notes
Use a large picture, high-contrast word cards and real actions where appropriate. Never require a learner to disclose disability or touch another person. Teach singular/plural by repeating two examples; avoid complex grammar explanations.

# Unit 3 review: Colours — Describe What You See

# Colours: Describe What You See
**Grade 1 English | Original MELA draft | NOT textbook-verified | Teacher review required**

## We will learn
- Recognise and say six common colour words.
- Use "**It is ...**" to describe a colour.
- Ask and answer "**What colour is it?**"

## Words
red · blue · green · yellow · black · white · colour

## Warm-up (3 minutes)
Show six labelled colour cards. Say each word slowly: **red, blue, green, yellow, black, white**. Children repeat, point, or choose the matching printed word. The cards should show both text and colour so colour alone is never the only clue.

## Listen and say (6 minutes)
Hold a labelled red circle.
- **Teacher:** What colour is it?
- **Class:** It is red.

Show a picture with a labelled green leaf.
- **Teacher:** What colour is the leaf in this picture?
- **Class:** It is green.

Show a blue crayon and yellow paper. Repeat. Explain that the colour of a real object can change: not all leaves are green and not every sky is blue.

## Try together (6 minutes)
Pair a colour word with the correct card: **red / blue / green / yellow / black / white**.
Then children practise:
- "**What colour is it?**"
- "**It is blue.**"
- "**It is yellow.**"

Use a black pencil or paper, white paper, and coloured drawings if available. If not, use printed or hand-drawn colour labels.

## Your turn (6 minutes)
1. Complete: "What ______ is it?" (**colour**)
2. Complete: "It is ______." (use a displayed colour word)
3. Match each labelled object picture to **red**, **green**, **blue** or **yellow**.
4. With a partner, choose a card; ask and answer the question.

## Check your learning (4 minutes)
1. Which word names the colour of the red card? **red**
2. Ask someone about a colour: **What colour is it?**
3. Answer for the green card: **It is green.**

## Teacher notes
Ask children to speak English, but accept initial mother-tongue support. Do not assume access to crayons or printed colour pictures. For colour-vision differences, teach and assess using **written labels, position and oral words**, not colour-only discrimination. This lesson is English vocabulary practice, not a test of colour vision.

# Unit 4 review: Counting — English Number Words One to Ten

# Counting: One to Ten in English
**Grade 1 English | Original MELA draft | NOT textbook-verified | Teacher review required**

## We will learn
- Say the English number words from **one** to **ten**.
- Match number words to small groups of objects.
- Say the number before or after a familiar number.

## Words
one · two · three · four · five · six · seven · eight · nine · ten

## Warm-up (3 minutes)
Count five stones or drawn circles with the group: "**one, two, three, four, five**". Touch or point to each item once. Use available objects only; pictures are enough.

## Listen and say (7 minutes)
Teacher shows numerals and says the words:
- 1 — **one**
- 2 — **two**
- 3 — **three**
- 4 — **four**
- 5 — **five**
- 6 — **six**
- 7 — **seven**
- 8 — **eight**
- 9 — **nine**
- 10 — **ten**

Point once to each dot as you count. A numeral is a written symbol; a number word is how we say or write it in English.

## Try together (6 minutes)
Draw three circles: ○ ○ ○. Count slowly: "**one, two, three**." Repeat with five and seven circles.

Call and response:
- Teacher: "**four**"; class: "**five**" (what comes next?)
- Teacher: "**seven**"; class: "**six**" (what came just before?)
- Teacher: "**ten, nine ...**"; class: "**eight**" (counting backwards).

Do a gentle movement or clapping count if comfortable; pointing at paper is equally valid.

## Your turn (5 minutes)
1. Match **2** with **two**.
2. Match **6** with **six**.
3. Fill in: one, two, ______, four. (**three**)
4. Fill in: eight, nine, ______. (**ten**)
5. Count four drawn circles, touching each exactly once. (**four**)

## Check your learning (4 minutes)
Teacher shows numerals 3, 7 and 10; child says "**three, seven, ten**". Ask: "What comes after five?" (**six**). "What comes before nine?" (**eight**).

## Teacher notes
This is **English-language number vocabulary**, not a substitute for the mathematics syllabus. Progress from oral practice to reading the words. Use small, countable groups and praise careful one-to-one counting. For learners who cannot speak comfortably, allow pointing or matching cards.

# Unit 5 review: Objects That We Have — I Have and She Has

# Objects That We Have
**Grade 1 English | Original MELA draft | NOT textbook-verified | Teacher review required**

## We will learn
- Name common objects at school and at home.
- Say what some objects are used for.
- Use "**I have ...**" and "**She has ... / He has ...**" in simple sentences.

## Words
book · pen · pencil · bag · cup · chair · bed · have · has

## Warm-up (3 minutes)
Show a real or drawn **book, pen, pencil, bag, cup** and **chair**. Ask: "What is it?" Say and repeat the English word. Pictures may replace objects so children are not expected to own or bring any item.

## Listen and say (7 minutes)
Teacher holds a book or its picture:
- "**This is a book.**"
- "**I have a book.**"
- "**We read a book.**"

Teacher points to a drawn cup:
- "**This is a cup.**"
- "**We drink from a cup.**"

Teacher points to a picture of a child holding a pencil:
- "**She has a pencil.**"
- "**We write with a pencil.**"

Point out the pattern:
- **I have** a pen.
- **He has** a bag.
- **She has** a book.

## Try together (6 minutes)
Match the object to a use:
- book → **read**
- pencil → **write**
- cup → **drink**
- chair → **sit**
- bed → **sleep**

Teacher reads a short story: "**Hana has a bag. In the bag is a book and a pencil. Sami has a cup.**" Ask:
- "Who has a bag?" (**Hana**)
- "Who has a cup?" (**Sami**)
- "What is in the bag?" (**a book and a pencil**)

## Your turn (5 minutes)
1. Complete: "I ______ a book." (**have**)
2. Complete: "She ______ a pen." (**has**)
3. Match a picture of a cup to **cup**.
4. Tell a partner or puppet: "**I have a ______.**" You can refer to a picture rather than an owned item.

## Check your learning (4 minutes)
Say the English name for three pictures. Complete "**He has a bag**." Tell what a book is for (**reading**).

## Teacher notes
Avoid activities that compare children's possessions or shame learners who lack school supplies. Use shared classroom materials or drawings. Model **have/has** without lengthy grammar rules. Invite learners to respond orally, by pointing or by writing one word.
